7 Years in Motion Design: Lessons From Cairo to Global Brands

AlBaraa Mahmoud · September 2026

I started as a Motion Graphic Designer working on campaigns and scenes, learning the fundamentals the slow way — tutorials, trial and error, a lot of projects that didn't work the first time. Seven years and 350+ videos later, working on campaigns for global brands from Cairo, a few things stand out looking back.

Craft is a floor, not a ceiling. Being good at After Effects gets you in the room. It doesn't get you a career on its own. What actually moved things forward for me was understanding the brief behind the brief — what a client or a Creative Strategist actually needed the video to *do*, not just what they said they wanted it to look like.

Mentoring junior designers taught me more than most client work did. Explaining why a transition works, or why a piece of feedback matters, forces you to actually understand your own instincts instead of just trusting them. If you get the chance to mentor early in your career, take it — it sharpens you as much as it helps them.

Location matters less than it used to, but it's not nothing. Building a career from Cairo aimed at global, remote-first work meant being deliberate about portfolio, communication, and English fluency in a way that might not occur to someone already sitting in a market with easy access to international clients. That deliberateness became a habit that's helped in every part of the work, not just the job search.

The tools will keep changing. Cinema 4D, Lottie, and now AI generation tools — none of that was a fixed skill set when I started, and none of it will be fixed five years from now. The thing that's stayed constant is caring about whether the final piece actually works, for the client, for the platform, for the person watching it. Tools are replaceable. That instinct isn't, and it's the only thing worth protecting as everything else keeps shifting under it.
