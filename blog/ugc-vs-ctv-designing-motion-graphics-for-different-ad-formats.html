UGC vs CTV: Designing Motion Graphics for Different Ad Formats

AlBaraa Mahmoud · September 2026

Most people outside performance marketing assume a "video ad" is one thing. After 150+ campaigns, I can tell you it's at least two completely different design problems, and treating them the same is the fastest way to waste a budget.

UGC ads live inside a feed, competing with real content from real people. The entire design goal is to not look designed. Text overlays that look native to the platform, not templated. Cuts that feel like a phone edit, not a production house. Sound that starts mid-thought instead of with a polished intro. When I design UGC creative, I'm actively working against my own instincts toward polish — because polish is what signals "ad" to a scrolling thumb, and that's the thing that gets skipped.

CTV is the opposite problem. It plays on a television screen, often alongside real broadcast content, sometimes without a skip button. That means production value actually matters again — clean typography at a readable size from ten feet away, color grading that holds up on a big display, pacing that respects a captive viewer instead of assuming a three-second attention span. The Arabyads CTV work I've done treats the format closer to a short TV commercial than a social ad.

The mistake I see most often — and made myself early on — is designing one version and just resizing it for every placement. A UGC-style ad on CTV looks cheap and out of place. A polished CTV-style ad in a TikTok feed reads as an obvious ad and gets scrolled past instantly. Every format has its own visual grammar, and motion design for performance marketing means being fluent in more than one.

If you're building out creative for multi-platform campaigns, budget time to actually redesign for each format, not just reformat. The platforms reward it, and so do the numbers.
