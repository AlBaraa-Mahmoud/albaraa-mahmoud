The After Effects Techniques I Use in Every Project

AlBaraa Mahmoud · September 2026

Seven years in, After Effects is still where 90% of my actual craft happens — the AI tools, the 3D work in Cinema 4D, all of it eventually funnels back into AE for the final assembly. Here are the techniques I reach for on nearly every project, regardless of the brief.

Expressions over keyframes, whenever possible. Anything repetitive — staggered text reveals, looping elements, linked properties — I build with expressions. It's slower to set up the first time and dramatically faster on revisions, which matter a lot when you're producing at volume for performance campaigns.

Precomps as organization, not just grouping. I precomp aggressively and name everything properly. On projects with tight turnarounds — and most of my work has tight turnarounds — a messy project file costs more time than it saves.

Track mattes for every text reveal. I almost never animate text opacity directly. A track matte gives me control over the wipe, the edge, the timing curve — and it's reusable across variants, which matters when you're producing five versions of the same ad for A/B testing.

Color managed from the start, not fixed at the end. Especially moving between UGC-style footage and CTV-grade output, I set up color decisions early rather than trying to grade everything into consistency in the final pass.

Essential Graphics for anything with brand variants. When a campaign needs the same template in ten colorways or five languages, I build it once as a Motion Graphics Template and hand variant control to whoever needs it — including myself, three revisions later at 11pm.

None of this is exotic. It's mostly discipline — building things in a way that survives revisions, because in performance marketing, nothing survives without revisions. The technique that's saved me the most time over 7 years isn't a plugin or a trick. It's building every project assuming it will need to change fast, because it always does.
