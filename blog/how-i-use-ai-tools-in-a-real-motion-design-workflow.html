How I Use AI Tools in a Real Motion Design Workflow

AlBaraa Mahmoud · September 2026

I've been in motion design for over 7 years, and the last two have changed how I work more than the previous five combined. Not because After Effects or Cinema 4D got replaced — they didn't — but because AI tools became part of the pipeline instead of a novelty sitting outside it.

Here's what my actual workflow looks like now. For B-roll or background plates I can't shoot or don't have budget for, I generate them in Kling AI or Higgsfield, then bring them into After Effects for compositing, color matching, and motion. Freepik AI and Adobe Firefly handle a lot of the still-image generation — textures, backgrounds, quick concept visuals I used to spend hours building from scratch. When a client asset comes in low-res or I need to blow something up for a CTV format, Magnific does the upscaling instead of me fighting with plugins that half-work.

None of this replaces the actual craft. AI gives me raw material faster. What I do with that material — timing, pacing, sound design, how a cut lands on a beat — is still 100% manual, still 100% After Effects and Premiere Pro. The tools didn't shrink my job. They shrank the boring 30% of it: sourcing, rough cuts, placeholder assets.

The biggest shift is in speed on performance marketing work. When you're producing UGC ads and CTV creatives for Meta and TikTok campaigns, turnaround matters as much as quality. I've worked on 350+ videos across 150+ campaigns, and the volume only works if the front-end (ideation, asset gathering, first drafts) moves fast. AI tools are what let that happen without cutting corners on the final output.

If you're a motion designer wondering whether to bother with these tools: the honest answer is that clients and creative strategists don't care what generated your B-roll. They care whether the final video performs. Learn the tools that get you there faster, keep your actual animation and compositing skills sharp, and treat AI as a stage in the pipeline — not the whole pipeline.
